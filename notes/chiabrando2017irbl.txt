Title: An Original Application of Image Recognition Based Location in Complex Indoor Environments
Author(s): Filiberto Chiabrando, Vincenzo Di Pietra, Andrea Lingua (Politecnico di Torino), Youngsu Cho, Juil Jeon (ETRI, Korea)
Group: Visual positioning against a 3D map / image database
Published: ISPRS International Journal of Geo-Information 6(2), 56, 2017 (open access, CC BY)


WHAT IT DOES
I read the whole paper. It describes the image recognition based location (IRBL) pipeline: a smartphone photo is matched against a server database, and the full camera pose (position plus orientation, six parameters) is computed. The interesting part is how the database is built. Instead of photographing the building, the authors laser-scanned it (Faro Focus3D X130: 114 scans, about 3 billion points, for the Bangbae metro station in Seoul; 111 scans, about 4.5 billion points, for seven floors of the ETRI building in Daejeon) and then generated synthetic RGB-D images from the coloured point cloud with their own tool (ScanToRGBDImage): 96 views per scan position (32 viewing directions at 0, 10 and 20 degrees of tilt, 2500x1600 px), about 9,700 images for the station and 10,700 for ETRI, taking 36-40 hours of batch processing. Retrieval uses the MPEG-7 CDVS visual-search standard (global descriptor ranking, then pairwise matching with a geometric check), modified so key points are spread over the image instead of concentrated in the centre. Pose estimation: SIFT matching, a fast DISTRAT geometric filter plus RANSAC (the paper reports the combination is about 10 times faster overall and about 100 times faster in the RANSAC step than RANSAC alone), then the 3D coordinates of the matched points are read from the reference image's depth channel and the pose is solved with DLT and least squares.
Evaluation: 30 query photos (20 at the station, 10 at ETRI) from three Samsung phones, with ground truth from photogrammetric resection against the LiDAR cloud (about 5 cm, 10 mgon). In the station, position discrepancies were below about 1.5 m (one gross outlier); 25% of the results were within 0.5 m, 65% within 1 m, 95% within 2 m; orientation precision was about 10 gon. At ETRI there were two outliers of more than 3 m in Y because few, close-by features were found, and two errors of more than 8 m in Z because different floors look alike and retrieval picked the wrong floor. Retrieval takes about 3 s.


RELEVANT TO MY WORK
Unlike the topological methods, this returns orientation as well as position from one photo, which is what an AR arrow needs to point the right way. It also shows an option for building the "map of known views" without a photo survey: synthetic views from a 3D model. And it documents exactly the failure I should expect: repetitive layouts (same corridor on every floor) cause wrong retrieval, so I will need a second cue (floor from a barometer, a sign or QR code, or the previous position) to disambiguate.


USE IT FOR
- Database design: synthetic reference views from a 3D model (here LiDAR; the paper says other sources such as SfM or a time-of-flight camera could work).
- Pose from a retrieved image plus its depth channel, and the outlier-rejection recipe (ratio/geometric filter before RANSAC).
- Evaluation: a photogrammetric ground truth for query images and reporting position and angle discrepancies.
- Failure-case discussion (similar floors, few close features, crowds).


CAVEATS
- A laser-scanning campaign and 36-40 hours of database generation per site is heavy for a low-cost project. The authors suggest cheaper sources of the 3D model but did not test them here.
- Small test (30 images), one query ignored because retrieval failed (score 2.54), and the conclusion's "sub-metric accuracy" is generous given the metre-level and multi-metre outliers in their own tables.
- Server-side MATLAB prototype; it says the real phone API and server are future work, so no on-device timing.
- 2017-era SIFT/CDVS features; a modern learned descriptor would change both the cost and the failure rate.
